Boots straight into video

Fullscreen playback about 15 seconds after power-on. No desktop, no login prompt, no boot text.

USB stick = content

Videos in the stick's root folder play as a looping playlist. Swap content with no laptop and no network.

Unplug it any time

The filesystem locks itself read-only on first boot, so cutting power can never corrupt the SD card.

4K HEVC in hardware

Hardware-accelerated playback up to 4K HEVC on Pi 4 and Pi 5. 1080p H.264 plays fine too.

Quick start

  1. Download pi-video-kiosk.img.xz from the latest release.
  2. Flash it to a microSD card (4GB or more) with Raspberry Pi Imager: choose Use custom and pick the downloaded file. Skip the OS customisation dialog and apply no settings.
  3. Copy your videos (.mp4, .mkv, .mov) onto a USB stick (FAT32 or exFAT), root folder, and plug it into the Pi. Not sure they'll play smoothly? Check and convert them here.
  4. Insert the SD card, connect the TV via HDMI, and power on.
The first boot takes 2 to 3 minutes. It resizes the filesystem to fit your card, then locks it read-only, rebooting a couple of times along the way. Every boot after that goes straight to video in about 15 seconds. A built-in welcome video plays if no USB stick is present.

To change videos: swap the USB stick contents and power-cycle the Pi.

Video format: read this if playback stutters

The Pi's hardware video decoder supports:

CodecMax smooth resolution
H.264 / AVC1080p
H.265 / HEVC4K (2160p)

4K H.264 files will play in slow motion. No Raspberry Pi can hardware-decode them. The video converter on this site checks your files in the browser and re-encodes anything problematic to 4K HEVC. Nothing is uploaded; the work happens on your own machine.

Prefer the command line? scripts/check-video.sh myvideo.mp4 in the repository flags problem files and prints the exact ffmpeg re-encode command.

How it works

  • Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit) built with pi-gen via a custom stage.
  • A systemd service runs mpv directly on KMS/DRM with hardware decoding. No X11 or Wayland.
  • Output is pinned to 1080p. 4K output drops frames on the Pi's GPU render path even when decode is hardware-accelerated, and TVs upscale 1080p invisibly. Your 4K HEVC files still decode in hardware and look great.
  • On first boot the root filesystem becomes a tmpfs overlay, so all writes go to RAM.
  • USB detection happens at boot: a stick with videos wins, otherwise the built-in videos play.

SSH access, persistent changes, and building the image yourself are covered in the README.

Hardware

Tested on Raspberry Pi 5. Should work on Pi 4 (same KMS/DRM and hardware HEVC path). Pi 3 and Zero 2 W will handle 1080p H.264 but not HEVC 4K.

You'll need: Pi and official power supply, microSD card, micro-HDMI to HDMI cable (Pi 4/5), and optionally a USB stick for content.
